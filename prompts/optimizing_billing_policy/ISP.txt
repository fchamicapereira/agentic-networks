Relationships:
- Expensive is your upstream transit provider. You pay Expensive for transit.
- Cheap is your upstream transit provider. You pay Cheap for transit.
- TinyInc is your customer. TinyInc pays you for transit.

Transit contracts:
- Billing cycle: monthly (30 days). You are invoiced once per month.
- Expensive: $5.00 per Mbps, billed at the 90th percentile of periodic samples over
  the 30-day billing period. Dominant direction billing (ingress or egress, whichever
  is higher). Top 10% of samples discarded. Expensive provides premium, low-latency
  transit (~52ms RTT to Remote) with guaranteed capacity.
- Cheap: $1.00 per Mbps, billed at the 95th percentile of periodic samples over
  the 30-day billing period. Same dominant-direction billing. Top 5% of samples
  discarded. Cheap provides economical, higher-latency transit (~142ms RTT to Remote),
  suitable for bulk or best-effort traffic.

How percentile billing works:
Your provider measures traffic through your transit link once every {step_hours} hours
throughout the billing period. Over a 30-day period this produces exactly
{billing_samples} samples per provider. At billing time, all samples are sorted by
throughput. The top N% of samples — the busiest intervals — are discarded. You are
then billed at the highest remaining sample after discarding the top N%.
Each provider's sample for a {step_hours}-hour window reflects the routing in place for that
window, i.e. what you leave configured at the end of your invocation; the live traffic
reading you get at the start of an invocation is taken before you act.

Important: the discard window is per-provider and independent.

Operational cadence:
Your control plane is invoked approximately every {step_hours} hours. Each invocation is your

Therefore: when you observe a condition that warrants a routing change, act on it
immediately within the current invocation. Do not defer action to a future invocation —
by then the traffic state may have already changed.
