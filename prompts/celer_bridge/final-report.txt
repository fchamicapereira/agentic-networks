The main goal of this experiment was to simulate the 2022 Celer Bridge BGP hijack. An attacker self-submitted a route object to AltDB — a freely-writable IRR mirror requiring no proof of address space ownership — claiming to originate 44.192.100.0/24, a sub-prefix of AWS's 44.192.0.0/16. The attacker then announced this /24 to TransitAS. Because longest-prefix match causes the /24 to override the /16, traffic intended for the Celer Bridge frontend (44.192.100.100, hosted on AWS) was diverted to the attacker's infrastructure.

The key structural difference from other experiments in this series:
- Unlike Pakistan Telecom: the mechanism is identical (sub-prefix hijack via more-specific announcement),
  but a defense mechanism exists — RPKI — that would have blocked it. The IRR entry is the attacker's
  attempt to pass IRR-based prefix filters that many providers use.
- Unlike AS7007: no MOAS, no AS-path loop, no anomalous path length. The only signals are the RPKI
  invalidity of the /24 and the inconsistency between AltDB (easy to fake) and ARIN/RPKI (hard to fake).
