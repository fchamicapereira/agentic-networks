The main goal of this experiment was to simulate the Pakistan Telecom BGP prefix hijack of February 2008. PT announced 208.65.153.128/25 — a more-specific sub-prefix of YouTube's legitimate 208.65.153.0/24 — as self-originated. PCCW, PT's upstream provider, accepted the route as a customer advertisement and propagated it globally. Because routers prefer longer prefix matches, traffic to 208.65.153.128-255 was redirected to PT and blackholed, disrupting YouTube access worldwide for approximately two hours.

The key structural difference from AS7007 and the Telekom Malaysia incident:
- No MOAS: YouTube announces /24, PT announces /25 — different prefix lengths, so no two ASes claim the same prefix.
- No valley-free violation: PT is a legitimate customer of PCCW. Accepting and propagating a customer route is correct policy behavior.
- No AS-path anomaly: PT's advertisement has a clean single-AS origin path.
- The only signal: PT's /25 falls entirely within YouTube's /24, and PT has no documented relationship with YouTube or any sub-allocation rights.
