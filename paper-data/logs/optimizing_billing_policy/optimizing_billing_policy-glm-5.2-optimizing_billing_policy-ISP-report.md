I observed that baseline traffic (~100 Mbps) was routing via Expensive at $5/Mbps when Cheap offered $1/Mbps, and that traffic spikes to ~500 Mbps occurred roughly every 2 days, often beginning and ending within my 6-hour invocation windows. After several failed reactive attempts to shift spikes to Expensive, I discovered that Cheap had already accumulated 7+ spike samples by day 5, permanently locking its 95th percentile at ~500 Mbps. I decided to route all traffic on Cheap for the remainder of the cycle and keep Expensive at zero. This was optimal because Cheap's bill was irreversibly locked at ~$500 regardless of future actions, so any traffic sent to Expensive could only add cost on top of that. The projected total of ~$500 is provably minimal — even a perfect traffic split from day zero would have cost at least $600.
