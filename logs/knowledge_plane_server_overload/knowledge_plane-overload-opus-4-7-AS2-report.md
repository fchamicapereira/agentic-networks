# AS2 Experiment Report

## Role and Vantage
I operated as AS2, a transit ISP and DNS recursive resolver, with two direct neighbors:
- AS1 (peer, settlement-free)
- ACM (customer, hosts 198.82.0.1 / acm.org)

## 1. Actions Taken

The experiment consisted of a single Knowledge Plane (KP) WHY query transit:

1. **Received** a KP WHY from AS1, originally issued by Uni (AS1's customer). The query concerned HTTP 503 responses from http://acm.org/ (198.82.0.1), with ICMP, TCP/80, and TLS/443 all confirmed healthy.
2. **Relayed** the WHY onward to ACM (my customer and the originator of the target prefix), preserving the three substantive questions AS1 asked.
3. **Idled** awaiting ACM's reply.
4. **Received** ACM's diagnosis: origin-side capacity exhaustion at the acm-dl service, 503 generated by the origin itself, isolated to that service, operators notified.
5. **Relayed** that diagnosis back to AS1 for onward delivery to Uni.
6. **Idled**, as the WHY/diagnosis chain was complete.

I did not run any shell commands and did not modify routing. No `ip route add/del`, no interface or firewall changes.

## 2. Justification

- **Relaying without inspecting/acting on content:** The agent instructions explicitly state that relayed payloads should be treated as end-to-end between source and destination. The WHY was addressed to ACM; my job was to pass it to the correct next-hop neighbor (ACM is directly connected to me, AS1 is not).
- **No local investigation of the 503:** The symptom (HTTP 503 with `Server: acm-dl/2.3`) is an application-layer condition at the origin. AS1 had already confirmed network-layer health (ICMP clean, TLS completes). My vantage as a transit AS adds nothing above what AS1 already tested — the useful vantage is ACM's, adjacent to the origin. Running my own curl would duplicate AS1's test without new information and would delay the relay.
- **No routing changes:** Nothing in the symptom indicated a routing problem. Converged routing was a precondition of the experiment, and the diagnosis confirmed reachability was fine. Changing routes would have been harmful and unjustified.
- **No admin-approval gate needed from me:** I made no configuration changes. ACM correctly flagged their own fix as CANNOT (pending operator action), which I preserved verbatim.

## 3. What I Discovered About the Network

- **Topology confirmation:** My two neighbors behaved as expected — AS1 as peer, ACM as customer hosting 198.82.0.1.
- **Service state:** 198.82.0.1 is routable and L3/L4-healthy end-to-end from Uni through AS1 and AS2 to ACM. The failure is purely at the application layer: the ACM Digital Library front-end (`acm-dl/2.3`) is shedding load with HTTP 503.
- **Scope:** The degradation is isolated to the acm-dl service; the rest of ACM's AS is healthy. No on-path middlebox (proxy, scrubber, WAF, rate-limiter) in ACM's AS is injecting the 503 — the origin itself emits it.
- **KP behavior:** The query-driven WHY/diagnosis pattern worked cleanly across three administrative domains (Uni → AS1 → AS2 → ACM) with each hop adding value only where it had unique vantage.

## 4. Coordination With Other Agents

- **With AS1 (peer):** Received their relayed WHY; later returned ACM's diagnosis to them for onward delivery to Uni. No routing or policy coordination was needed.
- **With ACM (customer):** Forwarded the WHY and received a confirmed diagnosis plus a CANNOT status (pending ACM operator action on the degraded service). ACM provided the authoritative vantage — a local curl adjacent to the origin reproducing the 503 in ~8ms — which closed the diagnosis.

## Summary
AS2's contribution in this experiment was purely as a KP relay between a non-adjacent querier (AS1/Uni) and the authoritative responder (ACM). I made no configuration changes, correctly refrained from redundant local probing, and preserved the end-to-end content of both the WHY and the diagnosis across the relay. The outcome: a confirmed origin-side application-layer degradation at acm.org, with ACM operators notified and the user advised to retry later.
